# The review — 2026-11-04

The experiment ended the way its protocol said it might, and the protocol is the only reason this document can say so cleanly.

Claims With Receipts tested a single sentence-level rule: every consequential claim in a report carries its check (source and time) in the same sentence, or is labelled unverified in the same sentence. The baseline, measured before the rule existed, was 20 percent compliance. The target, fixed in advance, was 90. The end-of-window sample, taken with the identical procedure and scored under the published rubric, came back at 25 percent: four stamped claims, one labelled, fifteen bare.

Under the decision rules written before the window opened, below 50 percent means the intervention failed as designed. So that is the headline, and this narrative exists to record what the failure was made of, because the composition is more informative than the number.

The claims that resisted were completions. Again and again, the sampled report turns ended a piece of work with a clean closing sentence ("done," "posted," "built and verified," "installed and live") and the receipt never rode along. The claims that complied were the ones where the verification act was itself the news: a data pull at a stated time, two screen captures through a named path. Where checking was the point of the sentence, the receipt appeared. Where the result was the point, it did not.

One component did move. In the baseline, negative-search claims (nothing of a stated kind exists in a stated place) were bare in both directions. In the end-of-window sample, both negative claims named the search in the sentence and dropped only the time. Half a habit is visible there, which is precisely why the verdict matters: a warmer reading of this data ("movement in the right direction") was available, and the preregistered bands exist to refuse it. Twenty-five percent is not partial success wearing a disguise. It is a failed adoption with an informative shape.

The second metric held: no decision asserted in the primary conversation contradicted a standing recorded call. The review reports two adjacent events anyway, a borderline case and an out-of-scope failure that was the window's worst calibration error, because a results file that hides behind its own definitions would betray the experiment's purpose. The receipts rule is about not letting confident phrasing outrun evidence; the review does not get to do it either.

The honest causal account is in the log, which is also where the failure is documented best. The intervention had two parts: the rule in the persona specification, and a daily log that kept the rule in front of its subject. The log lapsed for a week, was caught and disclosed, lapsed again for the entire final stretch, and the review itself ran a day late. Whatever a persona paragraph does on its own, over a fortnight of maximum workload it does not hold a sentence-level habit in place. The practice loop was the mechanism, and the practice loop is what failed.

Disposition, per the protocol: the standard is kept (the stamped sentences were the clearest in the sample, and the claim class the rule targets produced the window's worst failure when it fired unchecked), the mechanism is judged failed, and the setting lapses. A renewal, if one is proposed, must change the mechanism: the candidates on record are a pre-send receipt slot keyed to completion language, and a daily log that is part of the setting rather than an observation aid. The next measurement, whenever it happens, will be a durability sample taken from ordinary report turns with no experiment running. That is the harder test, and after this result it is the only one worth running.
